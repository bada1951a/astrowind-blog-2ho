## First, check the progress bar


To make the stakes concrete, consider an illustrative scene—not a real customer case or a claim about my personal repair history. I am the technician asked to advise someone whose iPhone shows the Apple logo and a progress bar that appears motionless. They are getting ready to leave, the phone holds the tickets and messages they need, and every extra button press feels like it might help. I do not begin by calling the phone broken. I ask what happened immediately before the screen stopped: Was iOS updating, was the phone being restored, or was data moving from another iPhone? I also ask how long the bar has looked unchanged.

In this imagined conversation, my first useful action is to slow the situation down. A bar can appear still while work continues, so I explain why repeatedly disconnecting power or pressing buttons can make it harder to tell what is happening. I do not promise that any step will preserve data, and I do not start with Restore. Instead, I separate the safer checks from the operation that erases the device, then walk through them in order. The rest of this guide follows that same technician’s reasoning: establish whether waiting is appropriate, choose the model-correct restart, try Update in recovery mode before Restore, and make the data risk explicit before anyone confirms an erase.


After an update, restore, or transfer from another iPhone, the progress bar under the Apple logo can appear still while the process continues. Apple says to connect the iPhone to a computer and use recovery mode if the bar has not moved for **at least one hour**. If work is still in progress, wait instead of repeatedly pressing buttons or disconnecting power.

## Step 1: Force restart for your model

A force restart is an option when a normal restart is impossible. It is different from Restore, which erases the device.

- iPhone 8 and later: Press and quickly release Volume Up, then Volume Down. Hold the Side button until the Apple logo appears.
- iPhone 7 and 7 Plus: Hold Volume Down and the Side button together until the Apple logo appears.
- iPhone 6s and earlier: Hold the Home button and the Side or Top button together until the Apple logo appears.

If the phone starts, back it up before trying further troubleshooting.

## Step 2: Choose Update first in recovery mode

If the iPhone remains stuck, connect it to a computer. Open Finder on a Mac, or the latest Apple Devices app or iTunes on a Windows PC. Use the button sequence for your model, but keep holding the final button until the recovery screen appears.

When the computer offers **Update** or **Restore**, choose **Update first**. Update attempts to reinstall iOS without erasing your data. Restore reinstalls iOS and erases the iPhone. Keep the device connected while the computer completes the update. If the download takes long enough for the iPhone to leave the recovery screen, let the download finish and enter recovery mode again.

Before entering recovery mode, I make a short preflight checklist with the person I am advising. First, we confirm that the bar has truly shown no progress for at least an hour after an update, restore, or migration; a shorter pause alone is not the threshold Apple gives for this situation. Next, we identify the computer and the app it should use: Finder on macOS Catalina or later, iTunes on older macOS, and the latest Apple Devices app or iTunes on Windows. I ask them to keep the iPhone connected and watch for the recovery screen rather than stopping at the Apple logo.

I also explain what a failed-looking attempt can mean. If the computer does not recognize the phone, or a required button is broken or stuck, repeating the same button sequence may not help; Apple says service may be needed when recovery mode cannot update or restore the device, or a button cannot be used. If the software download takes more than 15 minutes, the phone may leave the recovery screen. That does not mean the person should interrupt the download: let it finish, put the iPhone into recovery mode again, and then choose Update. I ask about backups before discussing Restore. Update is the safer first choice in Apple’s instructions, but no data outcome is guaranteed; Restore erases the iPhone. When irreplaceable data has no backup, I recommend pausing before the erase and contacting Apple Support or an authorized service provider. This checklist keeps the decision tied to what the screen and computer actually show, instead of guesswork or repeated button presses.

## Important data warning

Trying Update first does not guarantee that your data will be preserved. Restore erases the device. Check whether you have a recent backup; if the data is important and no backup exists, contact Apple Support or an authorized service provider before choosing Restore. Seek service if recovery mode cannot update the iPhone or a button is broken.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will Update definitely keep my photos?**  
No data outcome can be guaranteed. Try Update before Restore, and get help before erasing if the data matters.

**The progress bar has been still for 20 minutes. Should I use recovery mode?**  
Apple’s guidance uses at least one hour without progress as the threshold after an update, restore, or transfer. Give an active process time to finish.

**What is the difference between Update and Restore?**  
Update attempts to reinstall iOS without erasing data. Restore erases the iPhone and reinstalls iOS.
